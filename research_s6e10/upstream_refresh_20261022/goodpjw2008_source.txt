**What you just saw (Part 14).** Part 13 gave a ruler for the public board's noise. Part 14 asks the
sharper question behind every public-max file: can the public split be *read*, i.e. can you predict the
public score of a move you never submitted? And does the reading carry over to another file? Section 4c,
nine submissions, each prediction written down first:

1. **At home, it reads.** From five deviations measured on our 0.96180 stack we predicted a direction
   never submitted: **0.96183 predicted, 0.96182 public**. CV alone said 0.96177.
2. **It does not carry over.** The same moves on the public #1 file (0.96208) scored 0.96208 / 0.96208 /
   0.96207, not the predicted 0.96209 – 0.96211. The reason needs no labels: the two files share only
   **15%** of the pairs the move flips, so each file draws its own deviation.
3. **The public #1 is a public summit.** Read on its own terms, its public slope along the
   LightGBM-stacker axis is **0 ± 0.8e-5**, while the CV slope there is **+9.4e-5**. That is the
   signature of a file climbed on the public board.

The file this notebook submits is unchanged (the 10-15 twin, public 0.96187), and so is the title.

**Part 13, in short.** With the deadline close, everyone is choosing a final two by
public score. So we asked how much of a public score is the board's own noise, measured it on our
[Meta Stack page (Part 3)](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-meta-stack-on-public-oof-lb-0-96197), and turned it on this series. **Section 4b**:

1. **The rule.** The public board blurs the score gap between two files by
   **sd ≈ 0.003 × std of their percentile-rank difference** — two submission files are all it needs.
   On the 15 steps of Parts 1 – 8 it matches the directly measured sd at a median ratio of **1.0**.
2. **One step at a time, our public digits were mostly noise.** Only 3 of 15 steps moved the public
   score past ± 2 sd, and 14 of 15 CV gains were below what the board can resolve. The one real outlier
   is instructive: retraining on 10 folds raised CV +121e-6 and the public score −1e-5 (z = −3.1).
   That is the CV artifact Part 3 called out at the time, now a 3-sigma fact.
3. **In sum, the board agrees with CV.** Part 1 → Part 10: CV **+765e-6**, public **+780e-6**.
   Trust CV step by step, and the board only in aggregate.

The file this notebook submits is unchanged (the 10-15 twin, public 0.96187), and so is the title.

**Part 12, in short.** Part 11 ended with the twin stacks agreeing to 0.99990 and a claim: member
selection is exhausted, prior design is not. Part 12 is the week that tested every prior left,
plus the one dial the whole pool never turned — and it is published **without a new leaderboard
digit, because the evidence says there is none to be had here**:

1. **The last dial (section 3b).** Every TabPFN in the public pool runs `n_estimators=1`. Turning
   TabPFN-3.5's own ensemble to 4 builds **the strongest honest single model of this series**:
   OOF 0.961491 (+0.00042 over the same inputs at n_est=1, all five folds up; LB 0.96110 alone).
   Added to the 245-member stack it is worth **±0.000000**. The stacker, averaging sixteen
   n_est=1 TabPFN columns, had already built this ensemble implicitly. 4 → 8 adds +0.00002: saturated.
2. **The mirage, paid for (section 4).** Pseudo-labels from the public consensus looked like the
   series' best member (nested CV +0.000030, five folds of five) and lost 0.00004 on the public
   board: the teacher's test predictions carry every training fold's labels, and the CV gain was
   that leak reflected back. The one gate CV cannot police.
3. **The rest of the week's axes** — a pairwise-rank residual on the stack margin (0 trees),
   blend spaces (three readings, one value), the 0.96188 notebooks' partner recipe at every dose
   (flat), their digit only by 70% becoming their file (the
   [anatomy page](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-anatomy-of-a-public-0-96188-lb-0-96188)) —
   all closed, each with the submission that closed it.

## 2. Regularize families, not members

245 members are not 245 opinions: they are four families with different error structures and wildly different head-counts (157 GBDTs, 58 MLPs, 16 TabPFNs, 14 other foundation models). A single C treats a TabPFN column and the 90th GBDT column as equally trustworthy a priori. Giving each family its own penalty is five lines of code; the validation discipline is the part that matters.

# One L2 penalty per model FAMILY.  Scaling a column by s multiplies its effective penalty by 1/s^2,
# so "TabPFN x 3" means TabPFN columns pay one ninth of the default penalty.
def family(n):
    if "tabpfn" in n or n.startswith("pfn_"): return "tabpfn"
    if n.startswith("tfm_") or "tabicl" in n or "tabm" in n: return "tfm"
    if "realmlp" in n or "rmlp" in n or "mlp" in n: return "mlp"
    return "gbdt"
FAM = np.array([family(n) for n in names])
print({f: int((FAM == f).sum()) for f in ("gbdt", "mlp", "tabpfn", "tfm")})
SCALE = np.array([{"tabpfn": 3.0, "tfm": 1.5}.get(f, 1.0) for f in FAM])

def _fold(Zm, a, b):
    return b, LogisticRegression(C=0.003, max_iter=5000).fit(Zm[a], y[a]).decision_function(Zm[b])
def nested(Zm, seed):
    skf = list(StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=seed).split(Zm, y))
    o = np.zeros(len(y))
    for b, v in Parallel(n_jobs=min(5, os.cpu_count()))(delayed(_fold)(Zm, a, b) for a, b in skf):
        o[b] = v
    return roc_auc_score(y, o), [roc_auc_score(y[b], o[b]) for _, b in skf]

# The discipline that matters: the scales were chosen on seed 7 ONLY (a 12-point grid, Part 10's pool),
# then seeds 13 and 101 were run once each, untouched.  Three seeds, one story.
t0 = time.time()
for seed in (7, 13, 101):
    (s0, f0), (s1, f1) = nested(Z, seed), nested(Z * SCALE, seed)
    print(f"seed {seed:3d}   flat C=0.003 {s0:.6f}   family-reg {s1:.6f}   gain {(s1 - s0) * 1e6:+5.1f} x 1e-6   "
          f"{sum(b > a for a, b in zip(f0, f1))}/5 folds up")
print(f"({time.time() - t0:.0f}s)")

## 4b. New in Part 13: our own public scores, re-read against the board's noise

**The rule.** On our [Meta Stack page (Part 3)](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-meta-stack-on-public-oof-lb-0-96197) we measured how much the public board —
59,969 rows, 20% of test — blurs the score gap between two submissions. We took random 60k-row subsets
of the 700k out-of-fold rows (600 per move), then made eight public submissions whose scores we wrote
down before submitting. The board behaved like a random sample (joint test p = 0.22), and its
noise follows one line:

> **public sd of a score gap ≈ 0.003 × std( pct_rank(file B) − pct_rank(file A) )**
> — plus the rounding of both shown scores (sd 0.4e-5). For the private 80%: about 0.0012 ×.

It needs only the two submission files: no OOF, no labels.

**Our own history, re-read.** Parts 1 – 8 submitted 16 stacks, and we kept each one's out-of-fold
prediction and test file. They are now in [our dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/goodpjw2008/s6e10-goodpjw2008-extra-members-oof) (version 7, `series_stacks_p1_p8.npz`),
so the next cell recomputes everything. Our run:

| step | CV change ×1e-6 | public change | ± 2 sd (rule) | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 → 10-fold retrain | **+120.6** | **−10** | ± 85 | **−3.07** |
| 10-fold → P2 | −3.0 | **+180** | ± 143 | **+2.56** |
| P2 → P3 | +35.8 | +20 | ± 60 | −0.53 |
| P3 → P4 | +62.9 | +20 | ± 108 | −0.80 |
| P4 → P5 | +76.5 | +120 | ± 81 | +1.07 |
| P5 → … → P6 (2 steps) | +8.6, +1.6 | +40, +20 | ± 33, ± 25 | +1.91, +1.50 |
| P6 → … → P7 (3 steps) | +2.2, +83.3, +22.4 | −10, +50, +20 | ± 35, ± 124, ± 42 | −0.72, −0.54, −0.12 |
| P7 → … → P8 (5 steps) | +12.9, +21.5, +6.6, +18.6, +5.0 | −10, +30, +30, +10, +10 | ± 46, ± 43, ± 32, ± 32, ± 36 | −1.01, +0.40, +1.44, −0.53, +0.28 |

What it says:

1. **The rule holds on our own steps:** the sd measured on OOF subsets over the rule's sd has a
   median of 1.0 across the 15 steps, and these are additions of members, not the blends it was
   built on.
2. **One step at a time, the board could not see our progress.** 14 of 15 CV gains were smaller than
   what the board can resolve for their own step (± 2 sd). Only 3 public changes cleared ± 2 sd from zero, and the
   signs agreed 11 times in 15. The "seven sign agreements in a row" in our submission log around
   Part 7 was partly luck.
3. **The exception is the 10-fold detour.** An OOF prediction comes from one model trained on 80%
   (5 folds) or 90% (10 folds) of the rows. A test prediction averages all K fold models, which
   between them already see every row. So more folds lift the OOF score and leave the test file
   almost where it was. Part 3 concluded "fold count raises CV only"; the rule puts that at z = −3.1
   going in and +2.6 coming back out. Skip the detour (P1 → P2 directly) and z = +0.8. The remaining 14 steps have
   Σz² = 13.1 on 14 df, which is what a random sample gives.
4. **In sum, the board agrees.** P1 → P8: CV **+476e-6**, public **+520e-6**, ± 2 sd 238e-6. P1 → P10
   (our 245 stack, public 0.96186): CV +765e-6, public +780e-6. A gain several times the noise
   shows up; a single +2e-5 step is a coin.

**What it says about the final two** (public gap from this page's twin, ± 2 sd by the rule):

| file | rank corr with the twin | public gap | ± 2 sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| twin + 20% toward LGB (the CV-best tweak) | 0.99983 | −1e-5 | ± 3.2e-5 |
| our [Meta Stack file](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-meta-stack-on-public-oof-lb-0-96197) (public 0.96197) | 0.99797 | +10e-5 | ± 11.0e-5 |
| the 0.96206 public top (kirilltym / mayankraaz) | 0.99734 | +19e-5 | ± 12.6e-5 |

The CV-best tweak is invisible on the board, either way. The public-max files are 1 – 3 sd above
us *on the public rows*. But they were found by trying files against those same rows, and when we
replayed that climb on OOF rows where the truth is known, every step the board approved after the
real information ran out was worth **+2e-5 public and −1.4e-5 private**
([Meta Stack page](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-meta-stack-on-public-oof-lb-0-96197), part 4). So our two:
**(1) this twin — every weight chosen in CV; (2) at most one public-max file, knowing it is a bet
on 20% of the rows.** On the private 80% the noise is about 0.4× these bars: files this close will
reorder there regardless.

steps, lbs = list(S["names"]), S["public"]                     # our 16 submitted stacks and their public LB
pr = lambda v: (rankdata(v) - 0.5) / len(v)
O = {n: pr(S[f"oof_{n}"]) for n in steps}
T = {n: pr(S[f"test_{n}"]) for n in steps}
cv_ = {n: roc_auc_score(y, O[n]) for n in steps}
RND = 1e-5 / np.sqrt(6)                                         # both public scores are shown to 5 decimals
rule = lambda a, b: 0.003 * (b - a).std()                       # the rule: public sd of the gap between two files
rng = np.random.default_rng(13); rows = []
for (a, la), (b, lb) in zip(zip(steps[:-1], lbs[:-1]), zip(steps[1:], lbs[1:])):
    sub_ = []
    for _ in range(120):                                        # the rule, checked: sd on random 60k-row OOF subsets
        ip = rng.choice(len(y), 59_969, replace=False)
        sub_.append(roc_auc_score(y[ip], O[b][ip]) - roc_auc_score(y[ip], O[a][ip]))
    size = (T[b] - T[a]).std() / (O[b] - O[a]).std()            # the test move's length relative to the OOF move
    sd = np.hypot(rule(T[a], T[b]), RND)
    rows.append((f"{a} -> {b}", (cv_[b] - cv_[a]) * 1e6, (lb - la) * 1e6, np.std(sub_) * size * 1e6,
                 rule(T[a], T[b]) * 1e6, 2 * sd * 1e6, (lb - la - cv_[b] + cv_[a]) / sd))
R = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["step", "CV x1e-6", "public x1e-6", "sd measured", "sd rule", "+-2 sd", "z"])
print(R.round(2).to_string(index=False))
print(f"\nsd measured / sd rule, median over the 15 steps: {np.median(R['sd measured'] / R['sd rule']):.2f}")
print(f"steps whose public change cleared +-2 sd: {(R['public x1e-6'].abs() > R['+-2 sd']).sum()} of 15;  "
      f"CV gain below what the board resolves (+-2 sd): {(R['CV x1e-6'] < R['+-2 sd']).sum()} of 15")
z = (lbs[2] - lbs[0] - cv_["ens_v6"] + cv_["ens_v3"]) / np.hypot(rule(T["ens_v3"], T["ens_v6"]), RND)
rest = np.r_[z, R["z"].values[2:]]
print(f"P1 -> P2 directly (skipping the 10-fold detour): z = {z:+.2f};  the 14 steps without it: sum z^2 = {np.sum(rest ** 2):.1f} on 14 df")
print(f"P1 -> P8 in sum: CV {(cv_['ens_v18'] - cv_['ens_v3']) * 1e6:+.1f}e-6, public {(lbs[-1] - lbs[0]) * 1e6:+.0f}e-6, "
      f"+-2 sd {2 * np.hypot(rule(T['ens_v3'], T['ens_v18']), RND) * 1e6:.0f}e-6")

## 4c. New in Part 14: can the public split be read?

**The idea.** The public board is a fixed set of 59,969 rows. Move a file in some direction and its
public score changes by the CV change plus a deviation that belongs to those rows. The deviation is fixed:
it is not redrawn each time you submit. On 10-20 we measured it for five moves on our 182-member LR stack
(public 0.96180), writing each prediction down first ([Meta Stack page, Part 3](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/s6e10-meta-stack-on-public-oof-lb-0-96197)). If the
deviation is roughly linear in the move, it can be **read**: you can predict the public score of a
move you never submitted. All of this cell's inputs are in [our dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/goodpjw2008/s6e10-goodpjw2008-extra-members-oof) (version 8). The public
scores are measurements; everything else is recomputed below.

**1 — At home, it reads.** We moved away from TabPFN `n_estimators=4` (5%) and CatBoost CTR (15%), a
direction we had only ever measured the other way. CV says −3.4e-5 (0.96177). The five measured
deviations add +6.6e-5, so the prediction was 0.96183. Public on 10-21: **0.96182**.

**2 — On the 0.96208 file, it does not.** We applied the same moves to the public #1 file (mayankraaz's
and yuvrajlamba01's 0.96208):

| move on the 0.96208 file | predicted from the 0.96180 reading | public (10-21) |
|---|---|---|
| away from CatBoost CTR 5% | 0.96209 – 0.96210 | **0.96208** |
| away from TabPFN 5% + CatBoost 10% | 0.96211 | **0.96208** |
| away from TabPFN 5% + CatBoost 15% | 0.96211 | **0.96207** |

**3 — Why, without labels.** A small move changes AUC only through the pairs whose order it flips. The
public deviation is decided by which of those flipped pairs happen to be (positive, negative) on the
public rows. So for one move we count, over 30M random test-row pairs, how many of one file's flipped
pairs the other file flips too:

| two files | distance ÷ move size | flipped pairs shared |
|---|---|---|
| 0.96208 vs 0.96207 (nina2025) | 0.25 | 77% |
| 0.96208 vs 0.96206 (kirilltym) | 0.45 | 64% |
| our 0.96180 stack vs our twin | 1.65 | 29% |
| 0.96208 vs our Meta Stack 0.96197 | 1.78 | 26% |
| **our 0.96180 stack vs 0.96208** | **3.50** | **15%** |

Once two files are farther apart than the move is long, each draws its own deviation. **A public deviation
belongs to a (file, move) pair, not to a direction.** That is also why the public chain's "good directions"
do not carry over from one public file to the next. The private board, which is a different set of rows
altogether, is no exception.

**4 — Reading the 0.96208 file itself.** Three strong probes toward single learners and one check the
other way, all on that file, with predictions written first:

| move on the 0.96208 file | prediction | public (10-21) |
|---|---|---|
| toward our LGB stacker, 0.8 | 0.96202 (CV slope) | **0.96192** |
| toward our XGB, 0.4 | 0.96202 (CV slope) | **0.96195** |
| toward our TabPFN `n_estimators=4`, 0.45 | 0.96200 (CV slope) | **0.96193** |
| away from the LGB stacker, 0.6 | 0.96195 (CV slope) / 0.96203 (slope read from the probe) | **0.96199** |

We fitted the two LGB-axis readings together. **The public slope is 0 ± 0.8e-5 and the curvature is
2.5e-4**, against a CV slope of +9.4e-5 and a CV curvature of 2.1e-4 on our stack. The other two probes
agree: their public slopes are −4.4 ± 1.6 and +3.0 ± 1.8 (×1e-5). **The public #1 sits where the public
slope is zero and the CV slope is not**, which is how a file climbed on the public board should look.
There was nothing left for us to read on it: the best step we could compute was worth +0.2e-5, below what
the board displays, so we did not submit it. We do not claim a higher score, and the file this notebook
submits is still our twin.

**What it means for the final two.** A public-max file carries a deviation that belongs to that file and
those 60k rows. The private 80% is a separate draw, and the deviation we can expect there, given the public
one, is about zero. What the private board does see is the CV slope. Our picks stay the same:
**this twin (every weight chosen in CV) and at most one public-max file, knowing what it is.**

# 4c. Part 14: reading the public split.  Public scores are measurements (each prediction was written
# down before its submission); CV, deviations, flipped pairs and slopes are recomputed here.
OUR = "goodpjw2008-extra-members"
pr = lambda v: (rankdata(v) - 0.5) / len(v)
o, t = np.load(find("stack182_lr_lgb_oof.npz", OUR)[0]), np.load(find("stack182_lr_lgb_test.npz", OUR)[0])
A, At = pr(o["lr"]), pr(t["lr"]); a0 = roc_auc_score(y, A)        # our 182-member LR stack, public 0.96180
mem = {"lgb": (pr(o["lgb"]), pr(t["lgb"]))}
for m in ("tabpfn_ne4", "tabm_v4aux", "xgb_k10", "cat_ctr_k"):
    mem[m] = (pr(np.load(find(f"oof_{m}.npy", OUR)[0])), pr(np.load(find(f"test_{m}.npy", OUR)[0])))
measured = {"lgb": (1.00, 0.96154), "tabpfn_ne4": (0.60, 0.96152), "tabm_v4aux": (0.35, 0.96157),
            "xgb_k10": (0.55, 0.96153), "cat_ctr_k": (0.40, 0.96152)}       # 10-20: w toward m, public
Uo, Ut, dev = {}, {}, {}
print("(1) public deviation of each measured move, per unit of move (x1e-6)")
for m, (T, Tt) in mem.items():
    size = (Tt - At).std() / (T - A).std()                           # test moves are slightly shorter
    Uo[m], Ut[m] = size * (T - A), Tt - At
    w, lb = measured[m]; cv = roc_auc_score(y, A + w * Uo[m]) - a0
    dev[m] = (lb - 0.96180 - cv) / w
    print(f"    toward {m:11s} w={w:.2f}: CV {cv * 1e6:+7.1f}, public {(lb - 0.96180) * 1e6:+5.0f}, deviation {dev[m] * 1e6:+7.1f} per unit")

c = {"tabpfn_ne4": 0.05, "cat_ctr_k": 0.15}                            # (2) the home test: away from both
cv = roc_auc_score(y, A - sum(ci * Uo[m] for m, ci in c.items())) - a0
dv = -sum(ci * dev[m] for m, ci in c.items())
print(f"\n(2) home test: CV {cv * 1e6:+.1f}, read deviation {dv * 1e6:+.1f} -> predicted {0.96180 + cv + dv:.5f};  public 0.96182")

ids = te["id"].values                                                  # (3) shared flipped pairs, label-free
by_id = lambda f: pr(pd.read_csv(find(f, OUR)[0]).set_index("id").loc[ids].iloc[:, 0].values)
M208, twin_r = by_id("mayankraaz_surface_v4_096208_1021.csv"), by_id("twin_submission_1015.csv")
move = -sum(ci * Ut[m] for m, ci in c.items())
rng = np.random.default_rng(0); i, j = rng.integers(0, len(ids), 10_000_000), rng.integers(0, len(ids), 10_000_000)
dm = move[i] - move[j]
flips = lambda a: np.sign(a[i] - a[j]) != np.sign(a[i] - a[j] + dm)
fA = flips(At)
for name, a2 in (("our twin", twin_r), ("the 0.96208 file", M208)):
    print(f"(3) our 0.96180 stack vs {name:16s}: distance / move size {(a2 - At).std() / move.std():.2f}, "
          f"shared flipped pairs {(fA & flips(a2)).sum() / fA.sum():.3f}")
del i, j, dm, fA

d1, d2 = 0.96192 - 0.96208, 0.96199 - 0.96208                          # (4) the 0.96208 file, LGB axis:
a_ = -(0.75 * d1 + d2) / 0.84; b_ = (d1 + 0.64 * a_) / 0.8             # d = b g - a g^2 at g = +0.8 and -0.6
h = 0.05; cp, cm = roc_auc_score(y, A + h * Uo["lgb"]) - a0, roc_auc_score(y, A - h * Uo["lgb"]) - a0
print(f"\n(4) on the 0.96208 file: public slope {b_ * 1e6:+.1f}e-6 (+-8 from rounding), curvature {a_ * 1e6:.0f}e-6")
print(f"    on our stack, CV:      slope {(cp - cm) / (2 * h) * 1e6:+.1f}e-6, curvature {-(cp + cm) / (2 * h * h) * 1e6:.0f}e-6")

## 5. What Parts 11 – 14 taught us

- **When two collectors converge, stop collecting.** Everything that distinguishes the two biggest
  public-pool stacks is worth less than ±0.0000013 of CV, and their predictions agree to 0.99990.
  The honest next move was not a 250th member; it was measuring the agreement and saying so.
- **The stacker still had one dial left.** Not *which* members, but *how much each family is trusted a
  priori*: cutting the TabPFN family's penalty to a ninth is worth +3 to +7 × 1e-6 on every fold seed we
  tried. Member selection is exhausted; prior design is not.
- **An average of twins cannot beat both twins.** Our ½ + ½ file landed exactly on the better parent,
  not above it — the arithmetic of averaging noise. If you see a blend of near-copies *beat* its
  parents on the public LB, you are looking at luck, and the private board will take it back.
- **A member can be worth +0.0004 alone and nothing to the pool.** The stacker is an ensembling
  machine; any knob that only reduces a member family's variance (seeds, `n_estimators`, bagging)
  is a knob the stack already turned by averaging that family. New *information* moves pools;
  new *stability* does not.
- **The only CV gain the gate cannot police is a leaky teacher.** Nested folds, confirmation
  seeds, per-fold counts — all of it passed the pseudo-label member, and the public board
  refused it. If the teacher saw every label, distrust the student's CV on principle.
- **"Frozen published files" are only frozen if you re-host them.** Between Parts 11 and 12 the
  other twin's kernel re-ran; a deterministic recipe quietly produced a different file (LB
  0.96187 → 0.96186, rank corr 0.999994). This version pins the 10-15 original in our dataset.
- **Size every public gap before you believe it.** public sd ≈ 0.003 × std of the two files'
  percentile-rank difference. Across this whole series a single step was almost never visible on the
  board, yet the sum of twelve parts landed within 1.5e-5 of the CV's sum. Steer by CV, and audit by
  the board only in aggregate or for moves several times their noise.
- **A public deviation belongs to a file, not to a direction.** You can read the public split, and we
  predicted an unsubmitted move to within 1e-5. But the reading only holds on the file you read it on: a
  file 3.5 move-lengths away shares 15% of the flipped pairs. A file climbed on the public board ends where
  the public slope is zero, and the private board sees the CV slope.
- **Spend measurement submissions, not hope submissions.** This week's three measurement files
  (drop-the-48, the laptop TabPFN, the twin average) each answered a question no CV could. The ten
  daily submissions are an instrument; the final two are the bet.
