## 2. Trap 1 — treating 0 as a bad rating

The 13 service columns are integers 0–5. The tempting assumption is that 0 is the worst experience. The data says the opposite: **0 means “service not used”**, and non-use is heavily associated with Business cabin and long-haul travellers.

| Column | Positive rate at 0 | Positive rate at 1 | What a linear “0 is worst” model sees |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `Inflight wifi service` | **0.8873** | 0.3907 | completely wrong ordering |
| `Ease of Online booking` | **0.7076** | 0.4146 | wrong |
| `Online boarding` | **0.6204** | 0.1053 | wrong |
| `Gate location` | **0.5430** | 0.5144 | almost flat |
| `Departure/Arrival time convenient` | 0.4777 | 0.5060 | almost flat |

`Business` + `wifi = 0` is **0.9310** positive; `Eco` + `wifi = 0` is **0.8106**. If you pass these columns as LightGBM categoricals, you force an ordering on the levels and destroy the non-monotone “not used” regime. Keep them numeric; let the trees split, and add explicit `zero_count` / `non_zero_mean` summaries.

The next cell computes the rates from the actual training table and draws the two views that changed my mind.

## 3. Trap 2 — using the real dataset as extra rows

The real airline dataset is schema-compatible: 129,880 rows, same 21 feature names. The natural move is `pd.concat([train, original])`. It does not work.

| Statistic | S6E10 synthetic | Original real data |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean departure delay | **1.176 min** | **14.714 min** |
| Mean arrival delay | **1.134 min** | **15.091 min** |
| Zero departure delay | **90.78%** | much lower |
| Eco Plus share | **4.29%** | **7.25%** |
| Positive rate | 0.443573 | 0.434463 |

Adding 129,880 real rows to 699,635 synthetic rows moves the model toward a different delay/cabin distribution. The measured effect was **−0.000133** OOF (0.958330 → 0.958197): no gain, slightly worse. The external table is useful, but only as a *function* of the real world, not as extra rows.

The three transfer mechanisms that did work:

1. **Teacher** — a LightGBM trained only on the 129,880 real rows, scoring every competition row (competition AUC ≈0.9548).
2. **Smoothed target statistics** — real-world positive rate per categorical value, category pair and rating level, `alpha=20`, global-prior fallback.
3. **kNN-to-original** — mean real label of the 40 nearest real passengers in standardized numeric space (AUC ≈0.946 alone).

The teacher/target-stat/kNN representation moved OOF from **0.958330 to 0.958532** (+0.000202). The gain is real but modest; the larger gain came later from the model-family blend and the public stack.

## 4. Trap 3 — leaking the target through target encoding

Target encoding is the easiest way to make a CV score look better than the model. The failure mode is subtle: if the encoder is fitted on all training rows and then used to encode the validation fold, each validation row's category already contains that row's target. The model appears to generalise; it is reading the answer key.

The controlled demo below makes the mechanism visible. I create a **fake high-cardinality categorical** with 20,000 levels on an 80,000-row sample, then compare:

- **leaky**: encoder fitted on train + validation, then logistic regression;
- **fold-safe**: encoder fitted only on the training fold (with `alpha=20` smoothing).

The fake ID is intentionally extreme. Real competition combos have 4–6 levels, so the leak is smaller — but it is the same mechanism, and it is why the real encoder is fitted **inside each fold**, never on the full training table.

The production rule is stricter than “fit on the training fold”: for the RealMLP I used `sklearn.preprocessing.TargetEncoder` with an **internal cross-fitting splitter**, so even the training rows do not see their own labels through the encoder. The validation fold and the test set use the encoder fitted on the four training folds.


### CELL 9 code
# ============================================================
# Figure 4 | a controlled target-encoding leak
# ============================================================
rng = np.random.RandomState(SEED)
n = 80000
sample_idx = rng.choice(len(train), n, replace=False)
y_s = y[sample_idx]
fake_id = rng.randint(0, 20000, size=n)

skf = StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=SEED)


def target_encode(categories, target, alpha=20):
    gm = float(np.mean(target))
    d = pd.DataFrame({"v": categories, "y": target})
    g = d.groupby("v")["y"].agg(["sum", "count"])
    return (g["sum"] + alpha * gm) / (g["count"] + alpha), gm


leaky_auc, safe_auc = [], []
for tr, va in skf.split(fake_id, y_s):
    # leaky: encoder has seen the validation targets
    enc_all, gm = target_encode(fake_id, y_s)
    tr_e = pd.Series(fake_id[tr]).map(enc_all).fillna(gm).values
    va_e = pd.Series(fake_id[va]).map(enc_all).fillna(gm).values
    lr = LogisticRegression(C=1.0, max_iter=500)
    lr.fit(tr_e.reshape(-1, 1), y_s[tr])
    leaky_auc.append(roc_auc_score(y_s[va], lr.predict_proba(va_e.reshape(-1, 1))[:, 1]))

    # fold-safe: encoder fitted on the training fold only
    enc_tr, gm = target_encode(fake_id[tr], y_s[tr])
    tr_e = pd.Series(fake_id[tr]).map(enc_tr).fillna(gm).values
    va_e = pd.Series(fake_id[va]).map(enc_tr).fillna(gm).values
    lr = LogisticRegression(C=1.0, max_iter=500)
    lr.fit(tr_e.reshape(-1, 1), y_s[tr])
    safe_auc.append(roc_auc_score(y_s[va], lr.predict_proba(va_e.reshape(-1, 1))[:, 1]))

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 5))
vals = [np.mean(leaky_auc), np.mean(safe_auc)]
bars = ax.bar(["Leaky encoder\n(fitted on all rows)", "Fold-safe encoder\n(fitted on train fold)"],
              vals, color=[ORANGE, BLUE], width=0.55)
for b, v in zip(bars, vals):
    ax.text(b.get_x() + b.get_width() / 2, v + 0.015, "%.4f" % v,
            ha="center", fontsize=12, fontweight="bold")
ax.set_ylim(0, 1.05)
ax.set_ylabel("mean validation AUC")
ax.set_title("The leak turns a random ID into 0.77 AUC")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

print("leaky fold AUCs:", ["%.4f" % a for a in leaky_auc])
print("safe  fold AUCs:", ["%.4f" % a for a in safe_auc])
print("Insight: the fake ID has no real relationship with the target. The leaky encoder still scores ~%.2f." % np.mean(leaky_auc))


### CELL 10 markdown
## 5. Trap 4 — stacking the same model family

Three GBDTs with near-identical OOF scores look like diversity. They are not. LightGBM, CatBoost and XGBoost all split the same engineered features in similar ways; their rank correlation is high and their blend gain is small.

| Model | OOF AUC |
|---|---:|
| LightGBM | 0.958879 |
| CatBoost | 0.958852 |
| XGBoost | 0.958856 |
| Three-model rank average | 0.959903 |
| Four-member logit stack (+ RealMLP) | **0.960909** |

The first three are within 0.000027 of each other. Rank-averaging them buys about **+0.0010**; adding a different model family (RealMLP) buys another **+0.0010**. That is the actual lesson: **family diversity beats another seed or another GBDT parameter set**.

The 0.960909 four-member stack was still below the public-OOF consensus (0.962142), but it was the right local step: it gave the later stack four *raw* members to work with, instead of a pre-blended column.

### CELL 12 markdown
## 6. Trap 5 — the RealMLP 128-epoch trap

The first RealMLP run looked textbook-correct:

- 5 folds, seed 42;
- `n_epochs=128`;
- validation-AUC early stopping;
- `coslog4` schedule;
- hidden `[512, 256, 128]`;
- `embedding_size=5` and the six transformations.

It scored **0.957489** — below the LightGBM baseline. The early-stopping best epoch was only 23–53; the model plateaued almost immediately. More epochs, a lower learning rate and a larger batch changed almost nothing (fold 1 stayed at 0.9580–0.9581).

The public RealMLP recipe does something different, and the difference is not cosmetic:

| Choice | 128-epoch run | Public recipe |
|---|---|---|
| epochs | 128 | **6** |
| schedule | `coslog4` | **`lin_cos_log_15`** |
| models per fold | 1 | **8** (`n_ens=8`) |
| batch | 2048 | **512** |
| one-hot threshold | 9 | **`max_one_hot_cat_size=2`** |
| activation | default | **`silu`** |
| dropout | 0.15 | **0.05** |
| numeric twins | none | **17 categorical twins** |
| OOF | 0.957489 | **0.960791** |

The largest single change is not the learning rate. It is the combination of a **short, aggressively annealed schedule**, an **8-model internal ensemble**, and **categorical twins of the raw numeric columns**. The 17 twins let RealMLP learn embeddings for exact numeric values (age, distance, delays, rating levels) instead of only a smooth monotone function.

This is the most important meta-lesson in the notebook: **a “better” architecture with the wrong training recipe can be worse than a plain GBDT.** Always reproduce the recipe, not just the class name.

## 7. Trap 6 — the three kinds of fake public-OOF scores

A public OOF library is a gift, but not every CSV is a raw model. Three categories can inflate a nested CV without adding a real model:

1. **Blend-level files.** A file that is already a weighted average of several submissions has no single-model OOF. Feeding it to a stacker as if it were a member makes the meta-learner look better than it is. The cdeotte 124 package contains one such file, `nj_01`; I used it only for the diagnostic 124 reproduction and **excluded it from the final stack**.
2. **Stack-of-stacks.** A column produced by a previous stack (for example `oof_stack245.npy`, `stack247`, or a re-hosted `0.96188/0.96190/0.96193` file) already contains weights fitted on OOF rows. Its nested CV is not honest. Exclude it, even if its single-column AUC is the highest in the pool.
3. **Public-LB probes.** Files whose weights were tuned by submitting to the public leaderboard at 1e-5 granularity. They can score 0.96197–0.96207 on the public split without a CV claim, and they are the most dangerous thing to place in a stack. They are not used here at all.

A fourth, subtler trap is **the same model counted twice**. The cdeotte package re-hosts members from several authors; the golem library repeats 14 of its 17 members. Deduplicate at rank correlation **> 0.9999**, and hard-restrict known-overlapping libraries to their genuinely new members.

The honest 198-member stack, with `nj_01` excluded and no blend/stack inputs, reaches **0.962136** nested CV; adding our own RealMLP gives **0.962142**. The 124-member number is **0.962056** when the blend is included — almost identical, which is itself evidence that the blend was not carrying the stack.

### What the public ladder actually looked like

| Pool | Members | C | Nested CV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| cdeotte 124 (includes `nj_01`) | 124 | 1.0 | 0.962056 |
| + TFM | 135 | 0.003 | 0.962104 |
| + golem 3 + artifacts 38 + forge 6 | 182 | 0.003 | 0.962106 |
| honest raw, + extra 17 | 198 | 0.003 | 0.962136 |
| + own RealMLP | 199 | 0.003 | **0.962142** |

The C search is part of the discipline. The 124-only pool prefers C=1; the 182/198 pool prefers C=0.003. Hard-coding C=1 because a public notebook used it would leave about 0.00008 on the table.

## 8. Trap 7 — believing a 1e-4 public-LB difference

The public split is about 60k rows. At AUC ≈0.962, the standard error of an absolute AUC estimate is on the order of **0.0006–0.0008**. For two highly correlated stacks (`ρ ≈ 0.999`), the standard error of their *difference* is roughly

```text
SE_diff ≈ sqrt(2) · SE_AUC · sqrt(1 − ρ)
```

which is about **3e-5 to 6e-5**, not 1e-4. A 0.0001 public move is therefore 2–3 standard errors of a very correlated comparison — not a model improvement, and certainly not a reason to choose a different C or a different member list.

This is why every decision in the notebook uses OOF:

- C is chosen by seed-7 nested CV;
- a learned combiner must beat equal-weight rank averaging by **0.0002** to be used;
- the public LB is read only after `submission.csv` is written;
- two final slots require rank correlation **< 0.995**, not a 0.0001 LB edge.

The next cell simulates the sampling noise directly, so the size of the “signal” is visible rather than rhetorical.


### CELL 17 code
# ============================================================
# Figure 8 | how much does a 60k public split actually move?
# ============================================================
rng = np.random.RandomState(SEED)
n_public = 60000
auc_target = 0.962
d = norm.ppf(auc_target) * np.sqrt(2.0)

auc_draws = []
for _ in range(300):
    yy = (rng.rand(n_public) < 0.443573).astype(int)
    score = rng.normal(loc=d * yy, scale=1.0)
    auc_draws.append(roc_auc_score(yy, score))
auc_draws = np.array(auc_draws)
se_abs = auc_draws.std(ddof=1)
se_diff_999 = np.sqrt(2) * se_abs * np.sqrt(1 - 0.999)

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(11, 5))
ax.hist(auc_draws, bins=28, color=BLUE, alpha=0.75, edgecolor="white")
ax.axvline(auc_draws.mean(), color=ORANGE, lw=2, label="mean %.5f" % auc_draws.mean())
ax.axvline(auc_draws.mean() - 1.96 * se_abs, color=GREY, ls="--", lw=1)
ax.axvline(auc_draws.mean() + 1.96 * se_abs, color=GREY, ls="--", lw=1,
           label="95% sampling band")
ax.set_xlabel("AUC on a 60k sample")
ax.set_ylabel("draws")
ax.set_title("A 60k public split moves by ~%.4f (1 SD); a 1e-4 difference is not a model verdict" % se_abs)
ax.legend(loc="upper left", fontsize=8)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

print("SD of absolute AUC      : %.6f" % se_abs)
print("SE of difference, rho=.999: %.6f" % se_diff_999)
print("95%% band width          : %.6f" % (2 * 1.96 * se_abs))
print("Insight: selecting a member on +1e-4 public LB is selecting noise.")

## 9. Trap 8 — engineering choices that silently cost hours

These are not modelling ideas; they are the things that make a “correct” notebook fail or take three times longer than it should.

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Run All dies on data load | Hard-coded `/kaggle/input/playground-series-s6e10` | resolve both direct and `competitions/` layouts with `glob` |
| `Import Notebook` hangs on “Adding data sources” | a stale embedded `kaggle.dataSources` block | remove `metadata.kaggle`; add inputs manually |
| kNN feature takes forever | 40k references × 700k queries with a bad metric/thread setting | Manhattan distance, batch 20k, and do not use `n_jobs=-1` in a sandbox that blocks joblib pipes |
| RealMLP rejects the matrix | NaN in a continuous column | fill the raw `Arrival Delay` with 0; keep `arrival_delay_missing` |
| RealMLP trains for hours | `n_epochs=128` + a schedule meant for a different budget | use the public 6-epoch `lin_cos_log_15` recipe, `n_ens=8` |
| OOF looks great, LB does not | target encoding fitted on all rows | fold-internal cross-fitting for TE and counts |
| Stack CV looks 0.9622, then collapses | blend-level / stack-of-stacks member | exclude them; deduplicate at rank corr >0.9999 |
| Submission assertion fails | wrong id order or 299,843 rows | assert against `sample_submission` and clip to [0.001, 0.999] |
| A 0.0001 LB move “wins” | public split is 60k rows | select by nested OOF; require 0.0002 materiality |

The training budget matters too. The first valid three-model CPU run took **54 minutes**; the RealMLP public recipe took **10.3 minutes**; the 198-member public stack took **about 25 minutes** including C search and LOO. If a step is much more expensive than its OOF contribution, it is a candidate to cut.

One more engineering discipline: **label every non-standard fold.** The public pool contains 15 ten-fold members and 5 seed-33 members. They are legitimate raw OOF, but a reader must be able to see that they are not seed-42 five-fold. C15 prints a `folds` column for exactly this reason.

## 10. Checklist and what I would do next

### What worked

| Change | OOF effect |
|---|---:|
| Keep ratings numeric; add zero-count / non-zero-mean | built into the 0.9583 baseline |
| External data as teacher + target stats + kNN | +0.000202 over competition-only |
| Three different GBDT families | +0.0010 over a single GBDT |
| RealMLP with the public recipe | +0.0010 over the GBDT rank blend |
| Honest public-OOF stack, C selected by seed-7 nested CV | **+0.0012 over the four-member stack** |

### What did not work

| Change | OOF effect |
|---|---:|
| Concatenating the 129,880 real rows | **−0.000133** |
| 0–5 ratings as LightGBM categorical | destroys the non-use regime |
| Leaky target encoding | apparent gain, invalid CV |
| 128-epoch RealMLP + early stop | **−0.000841 vs competition-only baseline** |
| Family-wise scaling on this pool | +1e-6, noise |
| More GBDT seeds / near-identical members | ≈0 |

### The checklist I now apply before trusting a score

1. **Fold definition is frozen before any model.** Seed 42, same split for every member.
2. **Any target-derived feature is fitted inside the fold.** For RealMLP, use `TargetEncoder` with an internal CV splitter.
3. **0 is a service-use flag, not an ordinal bottom.** Keep ratings numeric and add summaries.
4. **External data is transferred as a function, not as rows.** Teacher / prior / kNN.
5. **A new model must add a family, not a seed.** Check rank correlation before adding another GBDT.
6. **A public-OOF member must be raw and paired with a test file.** Reject blends, stacks and LB probes.
7. **Meta folds use a different seed from member folds.** Seed 7 for the stacker; seed 42 for members.
8. **C is selected by nested CV.** Never choose C on public LB.
9. **A learned combiner needs a 0.0002 margin over the simple average.** Otherwise keep the simple average.
10. **The last check is format, not score.** 299,844 rows, `id,satisfaction`, id order, clip [0.001, 0.999].

### What I would do next

- **Do not chase 0.9630x.** The gap between 0.9621 and the public 0.9620x files is within the 60k-split noise; the private leaderboard can reshuffle it.
- **Add only material diversity.** A new model family with a genuinely different inductive bias is worth testing; another 0.9588 GBDT is not.
- **Keep the two slots structurally different.** A RealMLP-heavy public stack and a GBDT-only blend are a better pair than two 0.999-correlated stacks.
- **Leave the 245-member pool alone unless the missing sources are mounted.** The honest 198-member pool already clears 0.9620; padding it with blends would be a CV illusion.

The final public stack is not “one weird trick.” It is the same three ideas repeated: **clean semantics, fold-safe features, and model-family diversity** — plus the discipline to reject changes that only look good on a 60k public sample.
